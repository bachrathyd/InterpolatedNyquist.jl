# Which script makes which figure/table

Every figure, table and quoted number in the paper is generated by exactly one
script in `paper/scripts/studies/`. To experiment with a figure, edit the knobs
listed below in its script and rerun **only** that study:

```powershell
cd paper\scripts
julia --project=. -t auto generate_all.jl --only=s02_tolerance_error
julia --project=. -t auto generate_all.jl --only=s02_tolerance_error --force  # ignore caches
julia --project=. -t auto generate_all.jl --fast                              # coarse grids, all studies
julia --project=. -t auto generate_all.jl                                     # everything, production
```

Results are cached in `paper/data/cache/*.jls` keyed by resolution and (where
it matters) by a hash of the panel settings. **If you change a parameter that
is not part of the cache key, delete the cache file or pass `--force`** or you
will re-plot a stale grid.

Shared definitions live in `studies/systems.jl` (the showcase DDAE, the
4th-order benchmark, `sweep_grid`, `sweep_grid_dominant`, `mdbm_boundary`,
`stability_panel!`). Plot style, colour map and `write_macros` live in
`scripts/common.jl`.

## Two forms of the showcase characteristic function

`systems.jl` defines the same function twice, and which one a study uses is the
single biggest lever on its runtime:

* **`D_showcase_reduced`** — hand-derived: a flat scalar expression, **no
  determinant, no automatic differentiation**. This is the fast form and
  **every figure in the paper uses it**.
* **`D_showcase`** — `get_D_from_model(...)`: one call of the ODE right-hand
  side with ForwardDiff duals plus a 7x7 determinant. No algebra required of
  the user; roughly an order of magnitude slower per evaluation.

They agree to machine precision (checked in `s01_showcase.jl`, which computes
`D_showcase / D_showcase_reduced` and asserts it is a λ-independent constant)
and produce identical counts (asserted in the `s06` extraction block). Swap one
for the other in a study to see the difference; `tab_extraction` measures it on
one chart.

## The two settings that dominate cost

* **`ω_max`** — the paper's standard is `1e4` for every chart. Raising it is
  nearly free when the phase ripple decays fast (the 4th-order system: 1426 vs
  1330 evaluations from 1e4 to 1e6) and expensive when it does not (the
  showcase's delayed *velocity* feedback gives a 1/ω ripple: 1e4 → 1e6 costs
  ~60x, i.e. 48 s → 3014 s for one 100x100 chart). A too-small `ω_max` announces
  itself in the integer residual, so it is safe to tune empirically.
* **`tol`** — `1e-5` is the package default and is what the charts use.
  `1e-3`/`1e-4` are fine for a first exploration (a few boundary-adjacent
  pixels may be off by one; the traced boundary is unaffected). See
  `tab_tolerance` for the measured price.

## Panels that are NOT fast

Nine of the eleven gallery panels compute a 100x100 chart in well under a
second. Two do not, and the reason is the cost of evaluating `D`, not the
counting method:

* **29-DOF FEM bar** — a 29x29 linear solve per frequency, ~25k frequencies per
  point. Even with the matrix-determinant-lemma form (5.5x faster than the full
  determinant) the panel takes minutes.
* **50x50 dense determinant** — a dense 50x50 determinant per frequency; the
  grid is therefore 40x40 rather than 100x100.

Their measured times are in `data/gallery_timings.csv`, printed on the panels
themselves, and discussed honestly in the appendix.
