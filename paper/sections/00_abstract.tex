A fast method is presented for computing stability charts of linear autonomous time-delay systems. The number of unstable characteristic roots is obtained from the argument principle by tracking the phase of the characteristic function along the imaginary axis, with the phase slope evaluated exactly by forward-mode automatic differentiation. The winding number is either integrated as an ordinary differential equation by an adaptive high-order integrator or, without integration, obtained by discrete phase unwrapping: the exact phase increments between adaptively placed frequency samples are summed under an ODE-style step-size control driven by the exact slope. The integration-free march is 38 to 298 times faster on three benchmarks and more robust near the stability boundary. Since the parameter points are independent, its cost of 4 to 20 microseconds per point on one CPU thread divides almost exactly among CPU threads and, on graphics processors, in proportion to the number of threads up to the saturation of the device: on the fastest of four GPUs measured, a full-HD chart is computed in under a millisecond and an 8K chart in about ten milliseconds, fast enough to recompute it while a parameter slider is being moved. During the same sweep, the characteristic root closest to the integration line is estimated as a by-product; it makes the chart interpolable, showing both the number of unstable roots and the stability margin, and drives the multi-dimensional bisection method to trace the true stability boundary. The characteristic function can be extracted automatically from a simulation-ready right-hand side, including singular mass matrices. Retarded, neutral, distributed-delay, transcendental, high-dimensional and fractional-order case studies use one workflow, available in the open-source Julia package \pkg{InterpolatedNyquist.jl}.
